What this simulation represents

Inspired by Sagittarius A*, the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way, with a mass of about four million Suns. This is an idealized non-rotating model, not a reconstruction of its observed appearance. The simulation uses dimensionless horizon units; its playback is not calibrated to Sagittarius A*.

Distances are in horizon radii (Rₛ); local speed is a fraction of light speed (c). The horizon is at 1 Rₛ, the photon sphere at 1.5 Rₛ, and the innermost stable circular orbit at 3 Rₛ. Playback advances 12 Rₛ/c per second.

The marker represents a particle with negligible size and mass; its visible size is exaggerated. Its orbit follows the Schwarzschild geodesic equations. It fades near the horizon in distant-observer time, while the falling particle would cross in finite proper time.

This is a numerical snapshot visualization. Photon travel-time delays, black-hole spin, gas drag, tidal disruption of extended bodies, and full fluid dynamics are not modeled. Orange gas colors and turbulence are illustrative.
